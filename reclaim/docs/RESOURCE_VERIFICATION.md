# Resource verification status

Last updated: 2026-10-22

Reclaim only displays contact details that have been checked on an official
source. During the build, the official Ghanaian websites below could not be
opened from the build environment (its network policy blocked them), so no
phone numbers, emails or opening hours are published yet.

The candidates below came from search results and third-party pages. **They are
not verified and must not be published until someone checks them on the
organisation's own website or by contacting the organisation.** Once checked,
add them to `src/content/resources.ts`, set `contactsVerified: true` and
update `lastChecked`.

| Organisation | What needs checking | Unverified candidates seen during research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gaming Commission of Ghana (gamingcommission.gov.gh) | Current self-exclusion process: how to apply, what it covers, whether it applies to all licensed operators, how long it lasts, how it is reversed. Official phone, email and hours. | A toll-free line 0800 454 545 appears in third-party guides and reportedly in a Commission article on gaming and mental wellness (16 May 2025). The Commission reported 31 self-exclusion requests in Q1 2025 and 68 in 2024. Its contact page offers an enquiry form. |
| National emergency numbers (National Communications Authority / Ghana Police Service) | Which numbers currently work: 112 and whether 191 (police), 192 (fire) and 193 (ambulance) are still active. Police line 18555. | 112 is widely reported as the national emergency number. Reports conflict on whether 191/192/193 still work. 18555 listed on an MTN Ghana emergency contacts page. |
| Mental Health Authority | Official website address (mhaghana.com and mentalhealthgh.org both appear), helpline number, hours. | 0800 678 678 (listed by third-party directories). A temporary 2024 suicide-prevention line 020 681 4666 was reported by the press and may have ended. |
| Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection, "Help Line of Hope" call centre | Numbers, hours, whether it serves young people and mental health concerns. | 0800 800 800 and 0800 900 900 (from search summaries of mogcsp.gov.gh). |
| Ghana Police Service, DOVVSU | Current DOVVSU numbers for violence, threats and child protection. | 055 100 0900 (labelled rape crisis line on an MTN page). |
| Department of Social Welfare / Department of Children | Child safeguarding contact route. | None found. |

Also worth exploring with professional reviewers: Ghanaian counselling
services, university counselling centres, and any organisation in Ghana that
offers gambling-specific support. Do not list UK-only services such as
GamCare or the National Gambling Helpline as available to Ghanaian users.
